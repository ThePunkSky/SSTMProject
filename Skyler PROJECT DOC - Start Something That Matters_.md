Have you ever thought about running your own business? Working for yourself with no boss to answer to? Providing a product or service to people that helps make their lives better? 

In this unit, we will read *Start Something That Matters* (SSTM). This is Blake Mycoskie’s first-hand account of how he created the multimillion dollar company, TOMS shoes. He built TOMS from the ground up using his own creativity, hard work, and passion. His dream was not just to create a successful company, but also to help make people’s lives a little bit better. 

As you read SSTM, you’ll be thinking about how **Blake’s story of building TOMS** could apply to a **business of your own**. **Demographics \- Analyzing the Market for your Business**  
**Demographics** are important for businesses because they help with:  
**Product design:** Businesses can tailor products to meet the needs of different groups.   
**Marketing:** Businesses can tailor marketing efforts to meet the needs of different groups.   
**Business location:** Businesses can choose locations that are best suited to their target market. 

**Evaluate demographic**s \-Who are your customers/clients? Use the following questions to guide your thinking. 

***Brainstorm these questions about the demographics of your prime market will***  
***help you establish clear characteristics of the people you need to reach.*** (Demographic analysis) 

1. What age group(s) will be interested in your product/service?  
2. What do they do for a living?  
3. Will a particular gender want your service more than another?  
4. Is your product or service attractive to a particular ethnic or economic group of people?  
5.  Will only wealthy people be able to afford it?  
6. Does your ideal customer live in a certain type of neighborhood, and does this make a difference?

   
**For example,** if you are selling soap, you may believe that every dirty body needs your product, but you can't start with the entire world as your initial market. Even if you've developed such a universally needed item as soap, you need to identify a smaller, more targeted customer group first, such as children under eight who might like bubblegum- scented bubble bath. How many children in the United States are currently under the age of eight? How much soap will they use in a month or a year? How many other soap manufacturers already have a share of the market? How big are your potential competitors?

**DAY 3: Marketing Your Business-Create a Website**

**Create a website that describes your business and promotes your products and services. Change potential customers to committed supporters\!**

**Marketing** helps you focus on identifying your **competitive advantage**; it also establishes the **best ways to reach your potential customers** and what to say to them. Marketing describes the way you will **position your product or service within your target market.** Concentrate on the competitive advantages you identified. As you build your website, use the following questions to guide your thinking:

1\.    Will your product or service distinguish itself by its superior quality, its revolutionary features or its ability to make your customers happier than they've ever been in their lives?  
2\.    What will you say to persuade and convince customers that your product or service is better, more useful, etc?  
3\.   Who is your target audience? Consider the demographics of your customer base, so it appeals to those groups.  
4\.   What will your site look like? How will you make your site appealing to your target audience? Think about design (font, colors, images, navigation).

**Your website should include:**

1. Your company name & logo  
2. Company description and mission statement, and “your story” (“About” page)  
3. Product information presented clearly and creatively to target your audience(s)

**Consider the following when designing your website. Use the RUBRIC to make sure you have included all required elements.**

**Content:**  
**Quality:** Content should be engaging, relevant, and informative.  
**Freshness:** Search engines and visitors prefer fresh content.  
**Clarity:** Use clear and concise writing to convey information effectively.  
**Relevance:** Address the needs, interests, and challenges of your target audience.

**Navigation:**  
**Ease of use:** Make it easy for users to find what they're looking for.   
**Consistency:** Keep navigation consistent from page to page.   
**Intuitiveness:** Help users understand and interact with your website. 

**Design:**  
**Responsive:** Make sure your website looks good and works well on all devices.   
**Visuals:** Use strong visuals to establish brand identity and credibility.  
**Copyright:** Use copyright free images (Creative Commons)
